# Persona hypotheses

::: warning Assumptions, not interviews
These two fictional personas guide investigation. No customer statements or observed student behavior are claimed.
:::

## Persona hypothesis P1 — commuter buyer
Fictional example: Maya, a commuter student looking for a used calculator.

**Assumed goal:** compare price and condition and arrange pickup during an existing campus visit.

**Assumed difficulty:** uncertain availability and pickup arrangements can require extra messages.

**Context assumptions:** uses a phone between classes; may not have time for a separate trip.

**Proposed needs:** search, category/price filtering, clear condition, availability, and pickup information.

**Evidence boundary:** none of these needs were reported by an actual participant.

**What would disconfirm this hypothesis:** commuters prefer delivery or existing sellers regardless of location.

**Investigation:** recruit a consenting commuter and ask about their most recent used-item purchase, alternatives considered, and how pickup worked. Do not suggest a preferred answer.

**Planned task:** find an available calculator within a stated budget and explain its condition and pickup arrangement.


## Persona hypothesis P2 — student seller
Fictional example: Alex, a student considering listing a desk lamp and books they no longer use.

**Assumed goal:** find out whether a campus-focused listing can reach a buyer without too much listing effort; update availability after an item sells.

**Assumed difficulty:** missing condition or item details may prompt questions, while too many required fields may discourage posting.

**Context assumptions:** can describe the item's condition, but may not have a usable photo ready; time, photo availability, and willingness to list are unknown. A move or semester change is one possible context, not an observed pattern.

**Proposed needs:** a short form with clear required fields, optional photo support, a preview, edit controls, and available/sold status. The preferred balance between a brief form and a broader audience is unknown.

**Evidence boundary:** no actual seller interview or observed behavior supports this persona yet.

**What would disconfirm this hypothesis:** sellers prefer a larger general-market audience, shipping, or an existing service even if a campus form is shorter; sellers may also accept more entry effort if it improves listing quality.

**Investigation:** ask a consenting student about their most recent attempt to sell, how they chose a service, what information and photos they prepared, where they spent time, and what questions buyers asked. Ask neutrally; do not presume listing effort was a problem.

**Planned task:** use a sample item and supplied facts to create a listing, note any hesitation or missing information, inspect and edit the preview, then mark it sold. Record actual behavior only after a consenting participant completes the task.

No simulated quotations are used. Persona names and situations are invented and must not be represented as customers or interviews.
