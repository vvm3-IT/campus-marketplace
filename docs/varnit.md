## Source inspection
Original source: [Meta's Marketplace launch article](https://about.fb.com/news/2016/10/introducing-marketplace-buy-and-sell-with-your-local-community/), published October 3, 2016; page also displays April 23, 2020. Accessed November 17, 2026.

The article describes photo listings and filters for location, category, and price. That supports using a focused browse experience as a design precedent. It does not establish the current interface, student demand, or that our version will be easier to use.

## Audience decision
Use **NJIT student buyers and sellers** as the primary proposed audience. Keep one campus as the initial organizing context so categories and pickup details can be specific. This is a project direction based on the team's campus-marketplace idea, not a validated market segment.

A broader local audience might increase inventory, but would make campus relevance less clear. An affordability-first audience may be larger, but lower prices cannot be guaranteed.

## Positioning decision
Lead with **campus convenience**, with reuse as supporting value.

> Used essentials, organized around campus life and local pickup.

This statement describes the intended organization without promising lower prices, safer transactions, guaranteed sales, or university affiliation. Revisit it if actual student research shows inventory breadth or delivery matters more than proximity.

## Feature reasoning
1. The Meta source demonstrates a filtering precedent.
2. The commuter persona assumes a limited pickup window and budget; that assumption needs investigation.
3. Propose keyword, category, and maximum-price filters, with explicit condition and pickup context.
4. Verify filtering with a fixed listing set, including empty results. Separately observe a consenting student trying to find a relevant item. A technical pass would not prove campus demand.

## Scope decision
Research first. The future minimum slice is browsing/filtering, listing details, creating/editing listings, available/sold status, and pickup information.

Defer payments, shipping, ratings, and real-time messaging. Do not promise account verification until its implementation and meaning are defined. The current deliverable is published documentation, not a functioning marketplace.
